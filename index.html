Cyclone warnings that reach fishers early, in their own language

Our system reads India Meteorological Department (IMD) storm records, predicts when a cyclone is about to strengthen fast (rapid intensification, +30 knots in 24 hours), finds the coast it is heading for, and writes the warning SMS in that coast’s language. Below, it is replayed on every storm from 2019 to 2025 exactly as it would have run live.

How it works

From a storm record to an SMS in five steps

Read IMD’s record

Every 3 hours: wind, pressure, satellite CI number (Dvorak), position, recent change in wind.

Predict rapid intensification

A logistic-regression model trained on 250 storms (1982–2012) gives the chance of +30 kt in the next 24 h.

Keep the alert on

The alert switches on at the first flag and stays on while the storm is cyclone strength (34 kt+).

Find the coast

The Indian coastal state on the storm’s path picks the language: Odia, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Malayalam or Kannada.

Send, at most once a day

Only in the last 72 h before arrival, matching IMD’s first warning stage, so people get a few clear messages instead of dozens.

Model comparison

Tested once on 76 storms the models never saw (2019–2025)

All models use the same inputs and the same split by whole storm and by time. Alert thresholds were chosen on 2013–2018 storms. PR-AUC is the area under the precision–recall curve; guessing scores 0.06 because only 6% of times are rapid intensification.

Why fishers

  • Phones work only about 12 nautical miles out; many boats stay at sea for weeks
  • In Cyclone Ockhi (2017) about 3,000 fishermen were already at sea when the warning came
  • A short message in the reader’s own language is understood faster than an English bulletin
